# Editorial policy, data quality and coverage

## Product promise

Little Discoveries helps Sydney families find genuinely enjoyable, timely activities quickly. Prefer a smaller set of excellent, verified discoveries to a larger set of generic or uncertain suggestions. Research quality must be high enough that a parent can decide whether to attend **without needing to research basic facts again**.

## Discovery cycle

- Main editorial window: coming Monday–Sunday in the **Australia/Sydney** time zone. Look ahead for noteworthy special events while retaining the proper future release date.
- Sunday: generate approximately **40–50 review candidates** and source/quality/coverage notes.
- The release goal is about **30+ strong, approved and verified listings**, not 30 filler activities. Never invent content to hit a numerical target.
- Immediate event additions requested by the user are a separately approved, scoped batch and may be published immediately when explicitly directed.
- Keep a stable ID and source URL for every event; distinguish a recurring series from each dated occurrence. Merge multiple listings of the same occurrence.

## Editorial ranking

Give the greatest weight to:
1. **Novelty and child appeal**: unique workshops, festivals, exhibitions, animal encounters, cultural celebrations, STEM experiences, nature programmes, markets with genuinely family-oriented activities, creative experiences, special performances and flagship community events.
2. **Timeliness**: a specific session/date in the target week; exceptional experiences and one-off opportunities beat routine programming.
3. **Practical value**: truly free entry/participation, clarity of booking, venue/access, suitable ages and enough meaningful detail to attend.
4. **Trust and visual quality**: accurate primary source, real organiser event photography, documented attribution.
5. **Geographic/age coverage**: strong options in priority regions while maintaining reasonable breadth.

Order homepage discoveries as: **approved Editor's Choice first in the chosen order; then distinctive, time-sensitive events; then other quality opportunities; finally recurring sessions**. Do not rank a recurring storytime or regular museum entry above a significant one-off simply because its title matches a keyword. Recalculate against actual date and availability.

## Geography

First priority: **Eastern Suburbs**, especially Bondi/North Bondi, Vaucluse, Woollahra, Double Bay, Randwick, Maroubra and surrounding suburbs. Second: **CBD / city centre and Inner West**. Also cover Darling Harbour, The Rocks, South Sydney, Western Sydney, Lower North Shore, Upper North Shore, Northern Beaches, Sutherland Shire. Up to **two exceptional online** events.

Use the site's broad area taxonomy, not a free-text suburb filter. Preserve suburb and street address as separate detailed fields. Normalise legacy area labels deliberately; do not let data say “South Sydney” while the homepage filter only understands an incompatible taxonomy. Show a coverage matrix of **area × age group** and flag thin or empty cells without adding weak activities solely to fill them.

## Age groups

Use the user's newer editorial labels:
- Babies (0–1)
- Toddlers (2–3)
- Pre-schoolers (3–5)
- School Kids (5–13)
- Teens (13–16)
- Young Adults (16–18)
- Adults (18+)
- All Ages

The older public page still includes “Babies (1–2 years)” and only a subset of bands. Explicitly reconcile display/filter/data mappings before release; do not just assign “All Ages” to everything. Only use “All Ages” when primary-source information supports it, and record individual band eligibility where possible. Where boundary ages overlap or leave gaps, preserve the organiser's precise range and avoid claiming suitability not supported by evidence.

## Cost and exceptions

**Default discovery and release policy: FREE ONLY**, in line with the user's most recent corrections. Verify both the admission price and whether the advertised children's activity is actually free. Food, optional merchandise and clearly optional upgrades can cost extra if noted accurately. Avoid presenting a paid workshop inside a free-entry festival as free.

The older specification allowed up to two exceptional paid recommendations. That permission is **not** a standing override. Include a paid event **only after the user explicitly authorises an exception**, and label it clearly at review and publication.

## Primary-source verification and required facts

For each candidate record:
- Stable ID, original event title, organiser and primary-source URL
- Precise start/end dates and hours, timezone as applicable; distinguish session times from opening hours
- Venue, suburb, street/location if known, broad area; whether genuinely online
- Precise source age guidance + mapped site age bands
- Exact entry cost and conditions, booking requirement, live registration link where present
- Clear editorial description, three or more distinctive supported highlights for substantial events, planning notes that actually help families
- Event category, one-off vs recurring, last verification timestamp
- Original event image URL, licence/attribution where available, actual-image vs related-organiser vs appropriate-alternative flag; image load test
- Editorial priority, coverage contribution, candidate/approved/published/expired status, and approval history

Reject or hold events with unresolved **date, time, price, venue, source identity or actual age suitability**. Do not publish “See organiser”, “Confirm price”, “Check booking details” or generic circular descriptions as substitutes. When the organiser does not publish exact session times, only publish if the event genuinely has unrestricted operating hours that are established and correctly described.

No fabricated attendance availability. If capacity/booking cannot be confirmed, describe what the primary source actually says and link to its official booking page; do not imply a place is secured.

## Source hierarchy

1. Original organiser, institution, venue or council event detail pages, checked for the specific target date.
2. Organiser's official event ticketing page (Eventbrite/Humanitix etc.).
3. Trusted discovery sources (Eastern Suburbs Mums and other local community guides) for leads, **not** a substitute for primary verification.
4. Broad search only to discover more sources; de-duplicate and confirm through primary sources.

Start with councils, Sydney Opera House, museums/galleries, libraries, community centres and other high-quality Sydney event organisers. Monitor programme changes, not just recurring generic museum admission.

## Editorial output

Group approximately 40–50 candidates into **Strongest / Good / Coverage-useful**, with sortable source information, image proof and clear reasons. Show proposed six Editor's Choice picks as a recommendation only. Every review record must make it easy to approve, reject or edit. Missing details and image substitutions should be explicitly visible; incomplete candidates should be in a *hold* queue rather than misrepresented as publication-ready.

The user's selections prevail over agent scores. Approval is required even for excellent candidates.
